Kaggle’s Verification Methodology

Kaggle independently reviews benchmarks hosted on Kaggle Benchmarks. Each review checks whether a benchmark’s results can be interpreted the way its authors describe them, and re-executes the leaderboard on Kaggle’s own compute. This page describes the rubric we use, how we reach a verdict, and what a Verified badge does and doesn’t mean.

Overview

A leaderboard number is only useful if it measures what it claims to. Benchmarks can be undermined by wrong answer keys, graders that accept incorrect work, ambiguous tasks, broken environments or results that can’t be reproduced. Our reviews look for these problems before a benchmark carries Kaggle’s verification badge.

Reviews are independent of a benchmark’s authors. Authors see our findings before publication and can respond or fix issues, but the verdict is Kaggle’s.

Verdicts

Every review ends in one of three verdicts:

checkVerified

The benchmark can broadly be interpreted as described. Any errors we found don’t substantially affect results, and Kaggle’s re-execution matches the published scores.

warningFlawed

The benchmark has one or more substantive flaws that users need to know about to interpret results correctly. We list each flaw on the leaderboard.

helpNot enough information

We can’t review the benchmark: tasks, grading code or run logs aren’t available to us in enough detail to check.

Which benchmarks we review

We prioritise benchmarks by three criteria:

  • Impact: the benchmark evaluates tasks that matter, such as real professional work, safety-relevant behaviour or scientific capability.
  • Reach: the benchmark is widely cited in model launches and research, or is under-used relative to its quality.
  • Diversity: the benchmark covers a capability that other verified benchmarks don’t.

Authors can also request a review when they publish a benchmark on Kaggle.

Review rubric

The rubric has two parts. First we check that a benchmark can be reviewed. Then we look for defects that change scores.

Part 1: Reviewability

To be reviewed, a benchmark must give Kaggle access to:

  • The full task set, or a representative sample of at least 100 tasks when the main split is held out.
  • The grading code or rubric, including any reference answers.
  • A runnable harness pinned to a version, with the exact settings used for the leaderboard (prompts, tools, step limits, reasoning effort).
  • Per-task outputs or trajectories for the published results.

If any of these is missing and can’t be provided, the verdict is Not enough information.

Part 2: Score-affecting defects

We audit a stratified sample of tasks across every domain or category and classify any problems we find. Blocking issues lead to a Flawed verdict unless fixed; Note issues are published but don’t change the verdict.

Independent execution

We re-run every leaderboard result on Kaggle’s platform compute using the benchmark’s pinned harness. A result is reproduced when Kaggle’s score falls within the reported confidence interval, or within 2 percentage points where none is given. We run each configuration at least three times to measure run-to-run variance, and publish it.

info

Re-execution removes self-reported numbers from the leaderboard. Every score on a Verified benchmark is one Kaggle ran itself.

Review process

  1. IntakeWe confirm the benchmark meets the selection criteria and collect tasks, grading code, harness and run logs from the authors.
  2. Reviewability checkWe confirm we have everything in Part 1 of the rubric.
  3. Task and grader auditTwo reviewers independently audit a stratified sample and log every issue against the rubric, then reconcile disagreements.
  4. Independent executionWe re-run the leaderboard on Kaggle compute and compare scores.
  5. Author responseAuthors get our findings and 14 days to respond or ship fixes. Fixed issues are re-checked.
  6. Verdict and publicationWe publish the verdict, the issues found and the authors’ response on the benchmark page.
  7. Re-reviewAny new dataset or harness version triggers a new review before the badge carries over.

Example: AutomationBench

AutomationBenchZapier · dataset 1.0.6
checkVerified
  • Grading checks the final environment state with positive and negative assertions, with no LLM judge. Our known-bad and shotgun submissions all failed.
  • Leaderboard scores use a private held-out split; a 600-task public set is open source for local runs.
  • Runs that rerouted refused tasks to a fallback model are labelled “max with fallback” on the leaderboard.

FAQ

Does “Verified” mean the benchmark is perfect?expand_more

No. It means any errors we found don’t substantially change how results should be read, and that Kaggle reproduced the scores. Minor issues are still listed on the benchmark page.

Can a benchmark lose its badge?expand_more

Yes. A new dataset or harness version triggers a re-review, and we re-open reviews when users report credible issues.

How do I request a review?expand_more

Benchmark authors can request one when publishing on Kaggle Benchmarks, or by contacting the Kaggle Benchmarks team.

Who does the reviews?expand_more

Kaggle’s benchmarks team, with domain experts for specialised tasks. Reviewers don’t review benchmarks they helped build.
